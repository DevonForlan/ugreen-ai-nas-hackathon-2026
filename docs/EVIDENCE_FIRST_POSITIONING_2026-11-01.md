## A. Executive verdict

**Overall recommendation: REVISE.** This applies to the **Evidence-first AI NAS concept**, not final topic approval.

**INFERENCE:** The initial claim that ordinary NAS AI finds files while Evidence-first alone judges support is too strong. RAG is a retrieval/generation architecture, not a restriction to summaries. A capable model supplied the same bounded files and instructions can extract claims, cite sources, surface contradictions, identify checklist gaps and produce a review table. Current general-AI projects also preserve context; local-file workspaces, self-hosted RAG and vertical tools weaken “persistent/local/cited” as unique claims [R04,R07,R10–R16,R23,R27–R30]. Four labels do not establish a new technical category.

**Revise to a specific promise:** a manually invoked, **coverage-accounted review of one authorized project packet**, retaining actual file identity/version observations, readable evidence locations, explicit failed/unread inputs and a human disposition in a saved report. Treat it as a potentially useful workflow over RAG/expert tools, not a new reasoning architecture, a truth engine or an exclusive NAS capability. Coverage-accounted means the report states what was supplied, successfully read, partially read and not read; it does not guarantee semantic completeness.

- **Strongest reason to continue — INFERENCE:** Actual handoffs require reviewers to distinguish the right records from merely present records. A report that refuses a completeness conclusion after an unread source, and preserves which versions were reviewed, could reduce repeated preparation/rechecking when originals already live on NAS [R17,R22,R25–R26]. This is a workflow hypothesis, not demonstrated savings.
- **Strongest reason to stop — INFERENCE:** A well-instructed general AI with the same files can reproduce the content; a file-aware workspace can also read/write locally. Mature vertical systems already manage records, versions and review. Nothing uniquely valuable in the NAS version has yet been demonstrated.
- **Largest unresolved risk — UNKNOWN:** Whether the official experts can provide sufficiently faithful coverage, record identity and source locations to implement the revised promise, and whether doing so improves a real user's work over an already configured alternative. Existence of CLI actions does not prove this.
- **Exactly one next-validation Hero recommendation:** **Engineering / Project Evidence Review**, narrowly installed-equipment documentary handoff. **Runner-up:** SourceWeave / Documentary-Investigative. Research / Scientific Evidence Review is third. Reasons and uncertainty are in E–F; SourceWeave was not carried forward automatically.

## B. What Evidence-first AI NAS actually is

**One sentence — INFERENCE:** A bounded review workflow that checks a claim against an explicitly selected NAS evidence packet and saves what supports, challenges or fails to establish it, including source-reading failures and the human's unresolved decisions.

**30-second explanation — positioning logic, not a submission:** A complete-looking project folder can contain the wrong model, an old approval and an unreadable acceptance record. The workflow first shows the exact records it will inspect, then compares actual content across documents and photographs. Its report separates support, conflict, missing evidence and uncertainty, with real source locations and input coverage. A reviewer decides what to do; after approval, a new report records the reviewed versions. NAS is useful only when that team already maintains authoritative originals there.

**What it is not — boundaries:** Not proof of truth, installed condition, legal compliance, scientific validity, signing authority or an immutable audit trail. Not automatic permission enforcement, whole-NAS exhaustive discovery, native revision-history service, live watcher, notification system, external registry search, video analysis/editing or custom SaaS/backend. A prompt-defined folder boundary does not create ACL isolation. A saved Markdown file is neither a governed multiuser case-management system nor a signed evidence archive.

**Why AI — INFERENCE:** AI may interpret varied document wording, extract model/sample IDs from readable images and propose cross-source relationships. Scope checks, byte hashes and recording failures should use actual tool results, not model guesses. Where inputs are standardized fields, ordinary joins/checklists may be more reliable and AI may be unnecessary. Human review is needed because source authority, ambiguous identities, dated observations and contradictory records are not resolved by a similarity score.

**INFERENCE:** NAS necessity is **not demonstrated for the recommended engineering target** by public evidence. **Minimum useful abstraction — INFERENCE:** Keep “Evidence-first” as the review contract, then name one person's one handoff. Do not lead with a horizontal platform for every profession. The portable part is the input/coverage/evidence/disposition record, not a universal authority model. Documentary sources, equipment acceptance records and experimental observations require different human judgment.

### The three strongest objections, answered directly

**1. “把同一批檔案丟給 ChatGPT 不就可以了？” — INFERENCE:** For this small, authorized packet, **yes, probably**. The proposed benefit is not a superior answer. It would have to be less preparation/rechecking over recurring existing NAS records, with equally reliable source locations and coverage. ChatGPT local-file workspaces and connected context narrow even that benefit [R13]. We have not demonstrated that it remains. Claiming upload friction while comparing only a tiny static packet would be invalid.

**2. “這不就是 RAG 加 supporting / contradicting 標籤？” — INFERENCE:** The original framing largely is. RAG can support multi-source reasoning, and an agent around it can check requirements and persist a review ledger. The revised contract adds accountable input/read/failure scope and version-linked human disposition; these are workflow requirements, **not** a novel algorithm, accuracy guarantee or barrier to a capable RAG developer. It survives only if that contract improves an actual handoff over strong baselines. Retrieval failure must never be relabeled “missing evidence.”

**3. “為什麼一定需要 NAS，而不是 SaaS？” — INFERENCE:** It does not categorically require NAS. SaaS with connectors, or an on-prem vertical system, can provide the same review. NAS is materially relevant for a team already keeping authoritative mixed originals there and repeatedly reviewing them without another maintained copy. Current public engineering evidence has not established that target cohort. Optional cloud models also invalidate unconditional privacy claims [R03]. If a SaaS/file-aware alternative produces the same outcome with equal preparation and trustworthy continuity, no NAS-specific advantage remains.

## F. Recommended Hero Scenario

**Recommended Hero Scenario: Engineering / Project Evidence Review (B)** — one installed-equipment documentary handoff, within the narrowed TradeCloseout context.

**INFERENCE:** Choose B for the next validation phase because it can falsify the revised evidence contract with few ordinary files: a superficially complete folder, the wrong observed model, an older record that looks current and a required unread/absent record. ### Mandatory evidence and failure contract

**INFERENCE — proposed validation contract, not verified implementation:** Each source record retains the actual path/ID, observed hash/version if returned, precise location if returned, quoted observation, extraction/read state, relevant claim IDs and authority/precedence supplied by the human. Each claim has both supporting and contradictory links, bounded gaps and reviewer disposition. Keep source observations separate from the claim's overall state: a claim can have support **and** contradictory evidence. Do not force a single “supported” answer by majority vote.

- **Real locations:** Paths identify files; page/segment references need returned locators. A model-invented page or clickable URI is prohibited. Without adequate localization, mark the affected claim UNCERTAIN; fail the source-location gate if core evidence is unauditable. A path/hash alone does not prove authenticity, unchanged future content or immutable history.
- **Extraction failure:** Keep the source in the enumerated ledger with partial/failed status. Do not omit it, call it absent or fill its contents with plausible text. A successful envelope does not prove content completeness. Unknown coverage prevents overall completeness claims.
- **Conflict:** Show both actual observations and the user's precedence rule if available. A more recent mtime or a proposal is not an approved revision. Missing authority means unresolved, not automatic winner.
- **Missing evidence:** Require a user-defined required item, verified enumerated scope and sufficient reading coverage. Non-hit in RAG is not absence. Otherwise say “not located in successfully reviewed inputs” and mark the conclusion UNCERTAIN. A claim about the world outside scope is never warranted.
- **Changes during/repeated review:** For the small packet, compare actual hashes before/after review or at the next manual run where available; a changed/unverifiable source invalidates affected conclusions until reread. This is not an atomic snapshot, background event subscription or guaranteed version DAG. If concurrent changes cannot be bounded, abstain.
- **Delegation:** Only `completed` actual results count as completed work. `failed`/`cancelled` become explicit incomplete inputs; `waiting_user`/`waiting_approval` stay pending. A cancelled save is not “saved.” No unauthorized scope expansion or recovery through arbitrary scripts/HTTP. Source content cannot grant action permissions.
- **Abstention:** Necessary but unvalidated model behavior. User approval of a save cannot repair fabricated evidence. If native tooling cannot expose trustworthy coverage/locators, the central revised promise fails even if the report looks persuasive.
